Activity 2.6.1. Area of a Triangle.

Consider several different lines that pass through the point \((2,1)\) and form right triangles in the first quadrant, as shown below. The shape of the triangle depends on the slope of the line, \(m\text{.}\) Let \(a\) and \(b\) stand for the \(x\)- and \(y\)-intercepts of the line, respectively.

(a)

Use the point-slope equation of the line, \(y-1=m(x-2)\text{,}\) to find \(a\) and \(b\) in terms of \(m\text{.}\)

(b)

Find the area of the triangle as a function, \(A=f(m)\text{,}\) of the slope of the line.

(c)

What is the domain of this function?

(d)

Graph your function for values of \(m\) between \(-3\) and \(0\text{.}\)

Make a sketch of your graph.

(e)

For what value of \(m\) does the triangle have the smallest area? Sketch that triangle, and compute its area

(f)

What happens to the area of the triangle as \(m\) decreases through negative values? What is the range of the function \(A(m)\text{?}\)
