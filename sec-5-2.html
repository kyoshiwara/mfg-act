    Section 5.2 Logarithmic Functions

    In Chapter 4 we treated the logarithm as an operation; taking a log was the inverse operation for raising to a power, just as taking a square root is the inverse operation for squaring. Now we investigate the log function as the inverse function for the exponential function. We also solve logarithmic equations using two steps: we use the log properties to combine the logs into one log, then convert to exponential form.
    🔗
    Applications of log functions are not nearly as common as applications of exponential functions, so most students will find that thinking of logarithms as an operation is more useful than using a log function, but the added practice provided by this Lesson will probably be helpful.

    Activity 5.2.1 Making a Choice.

    Hick’s Law says that the time, \(T\text{,}\) required for a person to make a choice from a list or menu of \(n\) items is proportional to \(\log_2 (n+1)\text{.}\) A classic study measured the amount of time shoppers took to choose a flavor of jam from a display of 6 flavors and from a display of 24 flavors. Hick’s Law is used by restaurants and web-designers in making menus, and by survey companies in designing multiple-choice questionaires.
    🔗

    Aside
    Hick’s Law is an interesting logarithmic model from psychology, along with the Weber-Fechner Law. Why logs base 2? Psychologists hypothesize that the mind divides a list successively into halves.
    🔗
    1. Graciela is choosing an AirBnB for her next vacation. If there are \(n\) choices available, it will take her \(T\) minutes to make her choice, where
      \begin{equation*} T=5\log_2 (n+1) \end{equation*}
      🔗
      Complete the table and make a graph.
      🔗

      Aside
      1(a) Although \(n=0\) does not make sense for the application, point out that we can add that point to complete the graph.
